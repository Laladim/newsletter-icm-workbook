# New newsletter discovery workbook

Use this workbook before generating a publication profile. Answer in plain
language. Unknown is a valid answer. A guess disguised as a fact is not.

## Evidence labels

Label every material answer with one of these:

| Label | Meaning | Minimum record |
|---|---|---|
| Supplied fact | The owner states this is true about the planned publication | statement and speaker |
| Researched evidence | A source was opened and supports a specific claim | source, access date, claim, and limit |
| Interpretation | A conclusion drawn from facts or evidence | reasoning and uncertainty |
| Human decision | A choice that sets direction or authority | decision, decision maker, and date |

Do not merge these categories in one unlabeled summary.

## 1. Publication identity

- What is the working title?
- What lowercase kebab-case publication ID should name its folder?
- Who is accountable for the publication?
- Why should this publication exist now?
- What outcome would make the first ten editions worth producing?

## 2. Audience and reader problem

- Who is the primary reader? Describe one specific role or situation.
- What recurring problem, decision, or uncertainty does this reader face?
- What does the reader already know?
- What does the reader not need from this publication?
- Which adjacent audiences are secondary, and which are explicitly out of
  scope?

Human decision required: approve the primary audience and recurring reader
problem.

## 3. Editorial thesis and recurring promise

- What does the publication believe readers need more of, less of, or a
  different view of?
- What useful result should a reader receive in every edition?
- What can the publication promise repeatedly without overclaiming?
- What will make this distinct from existing newsletters in the same space?
- What evidence supports the positioning, and what remains an interpretation?

Human decisions required: approve the editorial thesis, recurring promise, and
positioning.

## 4. Topic boundaries and point of view

- Which topics are always in scope?
- Which topics are conditionally in scope?
- Which topics are prohibited or belong elsewhere?
- How much opinion is allowed?
- Which beliefs or principles guide selection and explanation?
- What would count as a misleading, sensational, promotional, or off-brand
  angle?

Human decision required: approve the point of view and topic boundaries.

## 5. Evidence sources and risk requirements

- Where may candidates come from?
- Which sources are primary authority in this domain?
- Which independent sources can corroborate a claim?
- Which interested, anonymous, generated, or weak sources need special labels
  or are disallowed?
- How fresh must evidence be?
- How are prior coverage and duplicate stories checked?
- Which claims require specialist, legal, compliance, medical, financial, or
  other qualified review?
- What uncertainty must be disclosed?
- What risk level is acceptable for unverified, disputed, predictive, or
  identity-sensitive claims?

Human decision required: approve the evidence standard and risk tolerance.

## 6. Voice and expertise posture

- How should the publication sound?
- What expertise can it truthfully claim?
- What expertise must it never imply?
- Which words, tones, or rhetorical habits should it avoid?
- How should fact, interpretation, and opinion appear to the reader?
- Which public examples sound right, and what exact qualities should be
  borrowed without copying their content?

## 7. Format, cadence, visuals, and reader action

- What recurring sections are required?
- What is the target length or reading time?
- How should sources appear?
- What action should the reader take after reading?
- What cadence is sustainable?
- Are visuals required, optional, or prohibited?
- If visuals are used, what purpose, accessibility standard, dimensions, and
  review checks apply?

Cadence is an editorial intention. It does not grant schedule or send
authority.

## 8. Ownership, review, platform intent, and publishing authority

- Who may authorize a routine shadow run?
- Where should local Markdown packages live?
- What exact local surface will the owner review first?
- Which human accepts the final publication profile?
- Who alone may schedule, send, activate, or publish?
- Is there a future platform preference? Treat it as intent, not an active
  integration.
- What would a supervised pilot need to prove before any live connection?
- What is the rollback route if the pilot fails?

Human decisions required: approve the final-review owner and all publishing
authority.

## 9. Metrics and learning

- What reader behavior would indicate usefulness?
- Which delivery, engagement, conversion, or reply metrics matter?
- When should each metric be captured so comparisons use the same window?
- What minimum sample is needed before changing strategy?
- Which qualitative signals matter alongside numbers?
- Who may write post-publish records, and what exact readback proves them?

Metrics inform future decisions. They do not replace human judgment about the
publication promise or acceptable risk.

## 11. Approval check

Before approval, confirm:

- every required human decision is explicit;
- evidence has a traceable source and access date;
- interpretations are labeled and do not pose as facts;
- unknowns remain visible;
- no tool, source, expertise claim, credential, or authority was invented;
- the first mode is `shadow`;
- the first delivery surface is local Markdown; and
- no platform write is authorized.

The owner approves the complete `publication-brief.md`, not this blank workbook
and not an agent's conversational summary.
