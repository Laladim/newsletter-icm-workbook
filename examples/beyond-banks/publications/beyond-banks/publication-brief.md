## Audience and recurring problem

- Primary audience: professionals who need concise context on nonbank credit
  developments.
- Secondary audience: informed readers following the same market.
- Out-of-scope audience: readers seeking individualized legal, credit, or
  investment advice.
- Recurring reader problem: important public developments are fragmented across
  sources and often lack a clear practical consequence.
- Reader knowledge and constraints: readers know the domain but have limited
  time to compare source quality and implications.

## Editorial thesis, promise, and positioning

- Editorial thesis: verified developments are useful when the edition explains
  what changed, what the evidence supports, and why the reader should care.
- Recurring promise: a concise, evidence-backed explanation of a material
  development and its practical consequence.
- Positioning: an independent explanatory briefing, not promotion, legal
  advice, credit advice, or investment advice.
- Point of view: evidence limits belong in the reader-facing explanation.
- What the publication must never imply: certainty, private knowledge,
  professional advice, or authority it does not possess.

## Topic boundaries and edition shape

- Always in scope: material public developments relevant to the stated reader.
- Conditionally in scope: opinion or forward-looking context that is labeled
  and grounded in verified evidence.
- Out of scope: rumors, private source material, promotional copy, personal
  advice, and unsupported forecasts.
- Required sections: what changed, why it matters, evidence limits, and sources.
- Optional sections: a short secondary item or watch point.
- Target length or reading time: a brief five-minute read.
- Intended reader action: understand the development and decide whether it
  deserves further attention.

## Evidence and risk

- Candidate sources: public notices, filings, opinions, and reputable public
  reporting.
- Primary authority: the public record that directly establishes the event.
- Independent corroboration: a source independent from the subject when a
  material interpretation needs support.
- Weak or prohibited sources: anonymous rumor, unattributed generated text, and
  inaccessible claims presented without qualification.
- Freshness and duplication rules: verify dates and check prior coverage before
  selection.
- Citation standard: every load-bearing claim points to an opened public source.
- Specialist review triggers: allegations, legal conclusions, individualized
  advice, and other high-risk claims.
- Approved risk tolerance: qualify or omit any claim that cannot be verified to
  the stated standard.

## Voice, expertise, and visuals

- Voice: concise, plain, analytical, and specific about uncertainty.
- Truthful expertise posture: explain opened evidence without claiming private
  knowledge or licensed advice.
- Expertise the publication must not imply: legal, credit, investment, or other
  professional advice.
- Visual requirement: optional.
- Visual purpose and inspection standard: use a visual only when it improves
  comprehension; inspect accessibility, relevance, and legibility.
