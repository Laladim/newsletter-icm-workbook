# Editorial contract for Beyond Banks

Source: the approved `publication-brief.md`.

## Reader and promise

- Primary reader: professionals who need concise context on nonbank credit
  developments.
- Recurring problem: important developments are fragmented and their practical
  consequence is not always clear.
- Recurring promise: explain one material development from opened evidence and
  state why it deserves the reader's attention.
- Positioning: independent explanatory briefing, not promotion or advice.
- Intended reader action: understand the development and decide whether deeper
  review is warranted.

## Voice and expertise

- Use concise, plain, analytical language.
- Separate fact, interpretation, and uncertainty.
- Explain only what opened evidence supports.
- Do not imply private knowledge, legal advice, credit advice, or investment
  advice.
- Avoid sensational, promotional, or certainty-heavy language.

## Edition anatomy

- Required: what changed, why it matters, evidence limits, and sources.
- Optional: one supporting item or watch point.
- Target: a five-minute read.
- Sources: link each load-bearing claim to an opened public source.
- Call to action: encourage appropriate further review, not a financial or
  legal action.

## Editorial selection

- Lead with the strongest verified material consequence for the primary reader.
- Use supporting items only when they add distinct evidence or context.
- Exclude rumor, private material, promotion, and unsupported prediction.
- Preserve unused items with a reason rather than forcing them into the edition.

## Quality bar

A strong edition is traceable, concise, specific about uncertainty, and useful
without overstating expertise. Any unsupported load-bearing claim, hidden
conflict, or implied advice blocks release.
